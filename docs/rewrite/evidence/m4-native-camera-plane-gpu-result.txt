Native Camera Plane and World GPU oracle — 8 October 2026
Opaque fill/depth and registered output qualification; full M4/M5 gate remains open.

Added native_camera_planes_and_world_box_match_analytic_pixels_and_depth to the
application's motion_spatial integration tests. The test authors real native
Rectangle -> Fill -> Camera Plane constructions alongside Box -> Material -> World,
commits a project snapshot and prepares it through Motion rendering. It also
renders each snapshot through the application's registered evaluate_scene_gpu path.
Both paths share the same Host; the direct adapter and registered renderer retain
their GPU state across cases. No alternate rasterizer supplies actual pixels.

Eight cases combine perspective (90-degree vertical FOV) / orthographic (4 m
vertical extent), viewport / 0.0123 m-per-canvas-unit sizing, and rear-plane
distances of 4 / 6 m. A smaller foreground plane is at 2 m. A blue World box,
0.6 x 0.6 x 0.2 m centered at [0,0,3], occludes both from the [0,0,4] camera.
Logical canvas is 200 x 100; output raster is 160 x 120, deliberately a different
aspect ratio. Image-graph assertion requires a single shared spatial pass.

Independent expected coverage derives only from authored rectangles and elementary
projection formulas. The front box face is independently at axial depth 0.9 m;
its projected half-width/height is 20 pixels in perspective and 9 in orthographic.
Every one of 153,600 pixel samples exactly matches expected opaque RGBA or clear.
Depth differs from declared plane/box distance by less than 2e-5 m; clear pixels
have no depth. Every registered-output pixel exactly equals the direct result.
No silhouette exclusions, relaxed tolerance or converted mesh bounds are used.

Result: passed on NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1070, NVIDIA driver 580.173.02, Vulkan,
RGBA16F, MSAA off; final registered/direct run 5.50 seconds.
